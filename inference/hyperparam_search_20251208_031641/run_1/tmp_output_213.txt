Studies have shown that the amount of information retained from reading varies greatly depending on several factors, including:

4. **Personal relevance**: The degree of personal connection or interest in the subject matter affects memory retention.

Another study published in 2018 by researchers at the University of Texas at Austin discovered that people tend to forget about material after a few days or weeks, but not immediately. This phenomenon is known as "forgetting curve." The forgetting rate depends on factors such as:

1. **Time elapsed**: Memories are more likely to be forgotten over time.
2. **Emotional intensity**: Strong emotions can enhance memory retention.
3. **Contextual influence**: The context in which information was learned affects its long-term retention.

These complexities make it challenging to predict exactly how much someone will retain from a particular text or source material.

1. **Engage with the material**: Make an effort to understand and connect with the content.
2. **Use active recall**: Test yourself on the material by summarizing it in your own words.
3. **Practice spaced repetition**: Review material at increasingly longer intervals to help solidify it in your long-term memory.
