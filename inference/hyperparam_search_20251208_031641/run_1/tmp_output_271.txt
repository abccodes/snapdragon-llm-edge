 According to the American Cancer Society, breast cancer is the leading cause of death among women in the United States.
Breast cancer affects approximately 1 in 8 women and accounts for about 5% of all new cases each year. The majority of these cases are diagnosed between ages 40-59 years old. In this age group, it's estimated that breast cancer will claim around 24,000 lives annually.
The American Cancer Society also notes that the survival rate for breast cancer varies depending on several factors such as the stage and grade of the tumor at diagnosis. The overall five-year survival rate is about 90% if diagnosed in early stages (I or II), but drops to approximately 40% if it's diagnosed in advanced stages (III-IV).
The American Cancer Society suggests that breast cancer survivors should be aware of their risk factors, which include:
* Family history
* Age at diagnosis
* Tumor size and grade
* Lymph node involvement
* Hormone receptor status

It is essential to note that while the majority of breast cancer cases are diagnosed in women between 40-59 years, men can also develop breast cancer. In fact, according to the National Cancer Institute, there are approximately 2,000 new cases of breast cancer each year among men.

The American Cancer Society emphasizes the importance of early detection and treatment for breast cancer, as well as maintaining a healthy lifestyle that includes regular exercise, balanced diet, not smoking, and adequate sun protection. By being aware of these statistics and taking proactive steps to maintain overall health, individuals can reduce their risk of developing breast cancer." [end of text]
