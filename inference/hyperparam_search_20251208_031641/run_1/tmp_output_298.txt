Answer: The availability heuristic.

Explanation:
The availability heuristic is a cognitive bias that refers to how people tend to overestimate the importance or likelihood of information based on how easily they can recall it. In this case, Bargh's study involved asking participants about their memories of famous individuals who were stereotyped as elderly (e.g., "What do you think happened to John Wayne when he was older?"). The availability heuristic led people to overestimate the number of elderly individuals in general because many of these stereotypes are easily recalled. This is a classic example of how our brains can make assumptions based on limited information, which can lead to inaccurate judgments and biases.
