**What are "cracks" in pavement?**

In its simplest form, a crack is a small gap or fissure in a material that has been weakened by external factors such as temperature fluctuations, chemical reactions, or mechanical stress. In the case of pavement, cracks can be caused by various reasons like:

* Freeze-thaw cycles: When water seeps into the pavement and freezes, it expands, causing the concrete to contract and create small cracks.
* Traffic wear and tear: Heavy traffic can cause the pavement to degrade over time, leading to cracks in both the surface layer and underlying layers of material.
* Environmental factors: Exposure to extreme temperatures, humidity, or chemicals can also contribute to crack formation.

**What happens when you step on a crack?**

When you step onto a cracked patch of pavement, several things happen:

1. **Micro-fractures**: The sudden impact from your foot causes micro-fractures in the concrete, which are small cracks that form at the surface level.
2. **Material degradation**: As you walk through the crack, you may notice that the surrounding material begins to degrade or break down more quickly than usual. This is because the stress and strain caused by your movement can weaken the bond between adjacent layers of pavement materials.
3. **Leakage**: If the crack is large enough, it may allow water to seep into the underlying layers, causing further degradation and potentially leading to more extensive damage.

**The physics behind "cracks" in pavement**

Now that we've explored what happens when you step on cracks in pavement, let's dive deeper into the physics behind this phenomenon:

* **Fracture mechanics**: When a material is subjected to stress or strain, it can undergo fracture, which is the formation of small cracks. In the case of cracked pavement, the stress caused by your foot movement can lead to micro-fractures that eventually grow into larger cracks.
* **Material properties**: The type and quality of materials used in construction play a significant role in determining their susceptibility to crack formation. For example, concrete with high water content or low strength is more prone to cracking than well-compacted material.
