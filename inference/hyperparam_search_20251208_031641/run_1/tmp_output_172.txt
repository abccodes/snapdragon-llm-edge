The discovery of fluoride in the late 19th century revolutionized dental care. Fluoride is a naturally occurring mineral that helps strengthen teeth and prevent decay. It was first used to treat tooth decay in children, but its use soon spread to adults as well. The widespread adoption of fluoridated water and toothpaste made it easier for people to maintain good oral health.

The 20th century saw the rise of modern dentistry, with advancements in materials like amalgam fillings and dental implants. However, despite these advances, many British people still struggle with bad teeth. There are several reasons for this:

1. **Lack of access to fluoride**: In some areas of Britain, especially rural ones, access to fluoridated water is limited or non-existent.
2. **Poor oral hygiene habits**: Many Brits do not practice good oral hygiene, such as brushing and flossing regularly, which can lead to tooth decay and gum disease.
3. **Sugar consumption**: While sugar has been reduced in recent years, many people still consume high amounts of sugary foods and drinks, making them more likely to develop cavities.
4. **Dietary habits**: The British diet is often high in processed foods, which are difficult for the body to digest and can lead to inflammation in the mouth.

To address these issues, the UK government has implemented various initiatives aimed at improving oral health. These include:

1. **Fluoride fortification**: Fluoridated water is now widely available in many parts of Britain.
2. **Public education campaigns**: The British Dental Association and other organizations have launched public awareness campaigns to promote good oral hygiene practices.
3. **Dental care services**: Many local authorities offer free dental care services, including fillings, extractions, and restorations.
