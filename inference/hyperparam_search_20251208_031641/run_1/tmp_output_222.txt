Organic farming is based on the idea that humans have a negative impact on the environment. The main goal of an organic farm is to minimize harm to the ecosystem and promote biodiversity. To achieve this, organic farms use various techniques such as crop rotation, companion planting, biological pest control (using beneficial insects), and physical barriers.

Organic farmers also rely on natural predators that help control pests in their crops. For example, ladybugs are a common predator of aphids, whiteflies, and other soft-bodied insects. Organic farming is not only good for the environment but also has several health benefits for humans.

Some of the key features of organic farming include:

*   **Organic fertilizers**: Instead of synthetic fertilizers, organic farmers use natural substances like compost, manure, or green manure to enrich their soil.
*   **Crop rotation**: Organic farmers rotate crops seasonally to maintain soil fertility and prevent pests from adapting to a single crop.
*   **Biological pest control**: Organic farmers rely on beneficial insects, such as ladybugs and lacewings, which prey on pests that can harm crops.

Organic farming has several advantages over conventional farming. Some of the benefits include:

*   **Improved soil health**: Organic farming promotes soil biota, improves soil structure, and increases nutrient cycling.
*   **Increased biodiversity**: Organic farming encourages the growth of diverse plant species, which helps maintain ecosystem balance.
*   **Reduced chemical use**: Organic farming reduces the need for synthetic pesticides, herbicides, and fertilizers.

Organic farming is a sustainable way to produce food that benefits both people and the environment. By adopting organic practices, farmers can reduce their environmental impact while promoting healthy eating habits. [end of text]
