 

Answer: Body Language

Amy Cuddy, a social psychologist at Harvard University, conducted an experiment in which she observed people performing different body language styles (power poses) and their corresponding effects on the brain activity. The results showed that people who performed expansive, open-body language were associated with increased alpha wave activity in the brain, while those who performed closed or rigid body language had decreased alpha wave activity.

This study has been widely cited as a demonstration of how power posing can influence our physiological responses and shape our behavior. Amy Cuddy's work on this topic has also led to the development of various interventions aimed at promoting positive body language and reducing stress in individuals, organizations, and communities.
