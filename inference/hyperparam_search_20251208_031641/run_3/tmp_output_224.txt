**Geography**

The Sahara Desert covers an area of approximately 9.2 million square kilometers (3.6 million square miles), spanning across several countries in North Africa. It stretches from the Atlantic Ocean to the Red Sea, covering parts of modern-day Algeria, Chad, Egypt, Libya, Mali, Mauritania, Morocco, Niger, and Tunisia.

**Climate**

The Sahara Desert is known for its extreme heat during the day and cold temperatures at night. The average temperature ranges from 10°C (50°F) in winter to 30°C (86°F) in summer. The desert's arid conditions are due to a combination of factors, including:

* Low humidity: Less than 20% relative humidity
* High evaporation rates: Water evaporates quickly into the air, creating an extreme dryness
* Limited precipitation: Average annual rainfall is around 25 mm (1 in) or less

**Unique Features**

The Sahara Desert has several unique features that set it apart from other deserts:

* **Sand dunes:** The desert's sand dunes are some of the tallest and most impressive in the world, with some reaching up to 100 meters (330 feet)
* **Oasis:** Despite its harsh conditions, the Sahara is home to numerous oases, which provide a source of water for humans and animals
* **Migration patterns:** The desert's extreme heat and cold create complex migration patterns among various animal species, including camels, goats, and gazelles

**Human Impact**

The Sahara Desert has had significant human impact throughout history:

* **Ancient trade routes:** The desert was an important route for ancient traders and travelers
* **Agriculture:** The desert's fertile soil is ideal for agriculture, particularly in areas like the oases of Libya and Egypt
* **Mining:** The desert is rich in mineral resources, including gold, copper, and iron

**Conservation Efforts**

In recent years, efforts have been made to conserve the Sahara Desert:

* **Protected areas:** Several national parks and protected areas have been established to safeguard the desert's unique ecosystems
