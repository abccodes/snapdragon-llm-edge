Heroin is a highly addictive opioid that affects the brain's reward system and releases feel-good chemicals like dopamine. It's often used recreationally for its euphoric effects, but also has serious health risks associated with long-term use.

The signs of heroin addiction can vary from person to person, but common symptoms include:

*   **Physical dependence**: The body adapts to the constant presence of opioids in the system.
*   **Withdrawal symptoms**: When a person stops using or reduces their opioid intake, they may experience uncomfortable and potentially life-threatening withdrawal symptoms.
*   **Loss of control**: As heroin use increases, individuals may find it difficult to stop or reduce their usage even when they want to.

If you're struggling with addiction, there are resources available:

1.  **National Helpline:** The Substance Abuse and Mental Health Services Administration (SAMHSA) offers a national helpline for people seeking help: 1-800-662-HELP (4357).
2.  **Local Treatment Centers:** Look for inpatient or outpatient treatment centers near you, which can provide comprehensive care and support.
3.  **Support Groups:** Joining a support group like Narcotics Anonymous (NA) can connect you with others who are going through similar experiences.
