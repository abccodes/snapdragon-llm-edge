Organic farming does not rely on synthetic chemicals like herbicides or insecticides. Instead, it uses a combination of these methods to manage pests and maintain soil health. Here are some examples:

1. **Crop rotation**: This involves rotating different crops in the same field to break disease cycles and reduce pest populations.
2. **Beneficial insects**: Organic farmers often use beneficial insects like ladybugs or lacewings, which prey on pests that can harm crops.
3. **Physical barriers**: Farmers may use physical barriers like row covers or fine-mesh screens to prevent pests from reaching their crops.

These methods are not only more environmentally friendly but also have several benefits for the farmer and the environment:

* They reduce chemical pollution and runoff into waterways
* They promote soil health and biodiversity
* They can be more cost-effective in the long run, as they don't require frequent use of synthetic chemicals

Overall, organic farming is a sustainable way to produce food that's not only better for people but also for the planet. [end of text]
