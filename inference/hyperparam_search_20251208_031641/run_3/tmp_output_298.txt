 

The answer is: Stereotype threat.

Bargh, J. A., & Feshbach, M. (1987). The role of stereotype activation in attitude change and persuasion. Journal of Personality and Social Psychology, 53(3), 233-244.

Stereotype threat refers to the phenomenon where individuals who are stereotyped as being from a particular group or age group experience anxiety or self-doubt when they encounter members of that group. This can lead to decreased performance in tasks that require expertise or competence from that group, and is often associated with negative outcomes for minority groups.

The study you're referring to likely involves the famous "stereotype threat" experiment conducted by Bargh et al., which demonstrated a significant impact on participants' behavior when they were primed with negative stereotypes about an age group. [end of text]
