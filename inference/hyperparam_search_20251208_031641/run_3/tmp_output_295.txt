Baumeister, R. F. (2003). Ego depletion and the self-concept. In The Oxford Handbook of Social Psychology (pp. 345-365).
According to Baumeister's research on ego depletion, individuals experience a decrease in their overall sense of self-worth when they engage in tasks that require mental effort, such as decision-making or problem-solving. This is because these activities deplete the individual's "ego" or self-concept.
The law of human psychology established by this study is: **Ego Depletion = Self-Concept Decrease**.

This means that individuals who are highly motivated and focused on their goals will experience a decrease in their sense of self-worth, making them more vulnerable to ego depletion. Conversely, individuals with lower levels of motivation or self-confidence may be less susceptible to ego depletion.
Baumeister's findings suggest that the extent of ego depletion is related to an individual's level of self-awareness and self-reflection. The more a person reflects on their own thoughts and feelings, the greater will be their sense of self-concept.

In summary, Baumeister's research shows that individuals experience ego depletion when they engage in mentally demanding tasks, which can lead to a decrease in their overall self-worth. This is reflected in the law of human psychology: Ego Depletion = Self-Context Decrease. [end of text]
