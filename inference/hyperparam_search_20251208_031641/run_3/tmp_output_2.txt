Veins are responsible for carrying deoxygenated blood back to the heart, while arteries carry oxygenated blood away from the heart. However, when we're not actively exercising or moving around much, our veins have a chance to relax and expand slightly due to gravity. This relaxation causes the vein walls to become thinner, allowing more oxygen-rich blood to flow into them.

As a result, the blue color of the veins appears because the deoxygenated (blue) blood is being drawn closer to the surface of the skin, where it's exposed to sunlight and other environmental factors that cause it to scatter shorter wavelengths of light. This scattering effect gives our veins their characteristic blue appearance.
