## Step 1: Early Days of Roadways
The concept of highways and roads dates back thousands of years. Ancient civilizations such as the Romans built extensive road networks for trade, commerce, and military purposes. These early roads were often unpaved and used by horse-drawn carriages or carts.

## Step 2: Development of Modern Roads
In the late 19th century, the first modern highways began to emerge in Europe and North America. The construction of the Transcontinental Railroad in the United States (1869) marked a significant milestone in this development. This railroad connected the east and west coasts of the country, facilitating trade and commerce.

## Step 3: Highway Act of 1925
The first federal highway law was enacted in 1925 by President Calvin Coolidge. The Federal Aid Road Act provided funding for the construction of a network of highways across the United States. This marked an important turning point in the development of modern roads, as it established a framework for future highway construction and maintenance.

The Highway Designation Act of 1945 had been passed earlier, but it was amended in 1956 to provide more specific guidelines for highway design. This act led to the creation of the Interstate Highway System (IHS), which would become a major focus of federal funding and planning.
