## Step 1: Understand the concept of aerodynamics
Aerodynamics is the study of how air interacts with objects moving through it. When an object moves through the air, it creates a pressure difference between the front and back of the object. This pressure difference generates an upward force called lift, which counteracts the weight of the object.

## Step 2: Analyze the factors that affect flying
For animals to fly, they would need to generate enough lift to overcome their weight and stay aloft. However, there are several factors that make it difficult for most animals to achieve flight:

- **Weight**: The more massive an animal is, the less efficient its wings will be at generating lift.
- **Wing shape and size**: A bird's wing shape and size determine how much lift can be generated relative to its weight. Larger wings produce more lift but also require a larger surface area for the same volume of air to flow over them.
- **Air resistance**: As an animal moves through the air, there is always some level of air resistance that opposes its motion.

## Step 3: Apply these factors to different animals
Considering the above principles, flying is not possible or feasible for most birds and mammals. However, some insects like butterflies and dragonflies are able to fly due to their unique wing structures and flight patterns.
