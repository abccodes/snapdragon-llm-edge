The answer lies in the world of geology and materials science.

When we walk, we're essentially walking over tiny particles called minerals or rocks that are embedded in the concrete or asphalt beneath our feet. These particles can be made up of various substances like quartz, feldspar, mica, or even glass. The size and type of these particles vary greatly depending on where they come from.

Here's a simplified explanation:

1. **Mineral-rich soil**: When we walk over mineral-rich soil, the tiny particles are carried into our shoes through friction with the ground. These minerals can be found in rocks like granite or basalt.
2. **Asphalt and concrete**: Asphalt and concrete have different properties that affect how they interact with these particles. Asphalt is a mixture of petroleum-based products, while concrete is made from cement, water, and aggregate (like gravel or sand). The size and distribution of the particles can vary depending on the type of asphalt or concrete used.
3. **Friction and wear**: As we walk, our feet rub against the surface beneath us. This friction causes the particles to break down into smaller pieces, which then get compacted by the weight of our body. Over time, these fragments accumulate at the bottom of our shoes.

Now, let's dive deeper:

* **Crack formation**: When a crack forms in the pavement or concrete, it can be caused by various factors like:
	+ Freeze-thaw cycles: Water seeping into the ground and freezing, expanding, and cracking the concrete.
	+ Traffic wear and tear: The constant pounding of foot traffic causes the surface to deteriorate over time.
	+ Environmental factors: Extreme temperatures, humidity, or chemical reactions can weaken the material.

The crack in question is usually a result of one of these factors. However, it's essential to note that cracks are not necessarily an indication of structural failure. In most cases, they're simply a sign of wear and tear.

**What happens if you step on cracks?**

When we walk over cracks, the particles at the bottom of our shoe can be broken down further by friction or other external factors. This process is called "fracture" or "degradation." The resulting fragments are then absorbed into the surrounding material, potentially leading to:

* **Micro-cracking**: Small cracks that may not be immediately visible but can still weaken the structure.
* **Material degradation**: The particles at the bottom of your shoe can break down further, releasing chemicals and contributing to the deterioration of the pavement or concrete.

In summary, when we walk on cracked pavement, it's a natural process involving friction, wear, and tear. While cracks may not be an immediate cause for concern, they can lead to micro-cracking and material degradation over time.
