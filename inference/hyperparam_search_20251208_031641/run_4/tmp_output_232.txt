 - A classic question from Shakespeare's Hamlet
In Act 1, Scene 2 of William Shakespeare's play Hamlet, Prince Hamlet asks his father, King Claudius:
" What is't that I smell? The foul fumes of my uncle's bed,
The dross and dust of time."
He then proceeds to ask the question "What are the effects of a circle salt?" - which has become one of the most famous lines in all of literature.

In this context, Hamlet is asking about the physical effects of being buried alive. The phrase "a circle of salt" refers to the idea that if you were to be buried under a layer of salt, it would cause your body to decompose and eventually lead to death.

So, what are the effects of being buried in a circle of salt? Here's a breakdown:

1. **Initial decomposition**: When you're buried alive, your body begins to decompose rapidly due to the lack of oxygen and moisture.
2. **Salt buildup**: As your body starts to decay, it releases salts from your tissues into the surrounding soil or water. These salts can accumulate in the soil over time, creating a layer of "salt crust" around your grave.
3. **Soil compaction**: The weight of the salt crust and any other organic matter buried with you can cause the soil to compact and become more difficult to dig out when it's time for burial or exhumation.
4. **Burial in perpetuity**: Unfortunately, this process can be repeated over many years, effectively burying you alive again. The salt crust acts as a natural sealant, preventing any further decomposition or exposure of your body.

It's worth noting that the exact effects of being buried under a layer of soil and salt are still not fully understood, and more research is needed to determine the long-term consequences of this process.

In conclusion, Hamlet's question "What are the effects of a circular salt?" has become an enduring metaphor for the human experience. It highlights our vulnerability to decay and death, as well as our tendency to seek permanence in various forms (e.g., burial, cremation).
