Diamonds are made of carbon, just like graphite and other minerals. However, the unique combination of factors in their formation makes them incredibly durable. Here's what sets diamonds apart:

1. **Chemical stability**: Diamonds have a very low reactivity with air, which means they don't react easily to oxygen or moisture.
2. **Crystal structure**: The diamond crystal structure is made up of tightly packed carbon atoms that are bonded together in a way that creates incredible strength and hardness.
3. **Hardness**: Diamonds are one of the hardest substances known, with a Mohs hardness rating of 10. This means they can withstand extreme pressure and temperature changes without breaking or deforming.

As a result, diamonds are incredibly resistant to scratches, cracks, and other forms of damage. They're also extremely rare, which contributes to their value and desirability.

In terms of how long diamonds last, it's not just about the physical properties of the diamond itself. The lifespan of a diamond depends on various factors, including:

* **Treatment**: Diamonds that have been treated with heat or chemicals can lose some of their durability over time.
* **Storage conditions**: Diamonds stored in air-tight containers or exposed to extreme temperatures or humidity can degrade faster than those stored properly.
* **Maintenance**: Regular cleaning and maintenance can help extend the lifespan of a diamond.

In general, diamonds can last for many decades with proper care. Here are some rough estimates:

* A well-maintained diamond that has been kept in a cool, dry place can last for 100 to 200 years or more.
* Diamonds stored in airtight containers and handled carefully can last for another 50 to 100 years.
