 by John L. Smith, published in the Journal of Experimental Biology on January 30, 2018.

This article presents a study that investigated how bumblebee flight patterns are influenced by wind and air currents. The researchers used data from an experiment to analyze the flight behavior of individual bees within a controlled environment. They found that:

* Bumblebees tend to fly in straight lines when there is no wind, but change direction if they encounter strong winds.
* Bees flying with stronger winds tend to have shorter flights and more frequent changes in direction than those flying with weaker winds.

The study suggests that bumblebee flight behavior can be influenced by the local wind conditions. The researchers propose a model for predicting how bees will fly based on their speed, distance from the source of food, and wind speed.

This article provides insights into the complex interactions between bumblebees and their environment, highlighting the importance of understanding these dynamics in order to develop effective conservation strategies or management practices. [end of text]
