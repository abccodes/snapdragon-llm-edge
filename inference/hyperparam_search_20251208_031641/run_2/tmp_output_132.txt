The United States entered World War I, but it withdrew from the League of Nations and joined the Allies after Germany's resumption of unrestricted submarine warfare. In 1941, President Franklin D. Roosevelt asked Congress to declare war on Japan; however, he also sought a declaration for the U.S. to enter into an alliance with Britain against Nazi Germany.
In June 1942, the United States declared war on Italy and in December 1943 it declared war on Germany. In August of that year, President Franklin D. Roosevelt delivered his famous "Infamy Speech" at the Yalta Conference, where he called for Japan's unconditional surrender as a matter of international law.
In September 1944, U.S. forces landed in Normandy and began their campaign to liberate Western Europe from German occupation. The Allies continued to push westward throughout 1945, eventually landing on the beaches of Normandy on D-Day June 6th, 1946. On August 15th, President Harry S. Truman delivered a televised address announcing that Japan had been officially declared at war.
In September and October of 1945 U.S. forces liberated Germany from its Nazi occupation; in November of that year the United States dropped atomic bombs on Hiroshima and Nagasaki, killing hundreds of thousands of people immediately, but not stopping the spread of nuclear weapons.
The end of World War II marked a new era for the world, as well as the beginning of the Cold War. The U.S., Britain, France, and China formed an alliance to prevent further conflict in Europe; however, this alliance was short-lived and ultimately failed due to disagreements over how to handle Germany's post-war occupation.
In 1947, a new international organization called the United Nations (UN) was established with the aim of preventing future wars. The UN Charter sets out its purposes as "to maintain peace and security" and "to promote cooperation among nations." In 1950, the U.S., along with other Western countries, supported the creation of South Korea to counterbalance China's influence in East Asia.
In 1961, a new treaty was signed between the United States and Cuba that provided for the removal of American troops from the island. The US also began its nuclear testing program at Nevada, which would eventually lead to the development of the hydrogen bomb.

The Cold War continued until 1991 when Mikhail Gorbachev's reforms led to the collapse of the Soviet Union. In the aftermath of this event, Russia and other former Eastern Bloc countries were allowed to rejoin the international community.
In 1992, a new treaty between the U.S. and Cuba was signed that provided for normal relations between the two nations; however, in 2009, President Barack Obama announced plans to normalize diplomatic relations with Iran.
