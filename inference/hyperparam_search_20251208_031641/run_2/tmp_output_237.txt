"Imagine if we were to suddenly reduce our orbit by just 10 feet," Dr. Rodriguez says. "The amount of solar radiation we receive would decrease dramatically. This is because a smaller distance from the Sun means less sunlight hits the Earth's surface."

Dr. Rodriguez and her team have run simulations using computer models to predict how this change in distance might affect global temperatures, sea levels, and weather patterns.

"Under these hypothetical conditions," Dr. Rodriguez explains, "the average global temperature would decrease by about 2-3 degrees Fahrenheit (1-2°C). This is a relatively small effect compared to the overall warming trend we're experiencing due to climate change."

However, this reduction in solar radiation would also have significant consequences for ocean currents and marine ecosystems.

"Sea levels would rise slightly as the warmer waters are more buoyant," Dr. Rodriguez notes. "This could lead to changes in coastal erosion patterns and potentially affect marine life that relies on specific temperature ranges."

Dr. Rodriguez emphasizes that these predictions are based on simplified models and do not account for many complexities, such as variations in ocean currents, atmospheric circulation, or the effects of human activities.

"Climate change is a complex system with many interacting factors," she says. "While this hypothetical scenario provides insight into how changes in Earth's distance from the Sun might affect our planet, it does not capture the full range of possible outcomes."

Dr. Rodriguez concludes that while reducing the Earth's orbit by 10 feet would have significant effects on global temperatures and ocean currents, it is unlikely to be a realistic or sustainable solution for mitigating climate change.
