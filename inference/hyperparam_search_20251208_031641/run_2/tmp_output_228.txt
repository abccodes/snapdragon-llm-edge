 by James Delaney
James Delaney, a British writer and activist, has written extensively on the topic of Agenda 21. Here's his take:

Agenda 21 is an international agreement that aims to address global environmental issues through sustainable development. It was adopted in Rio de Janeiro in 1992 at the United Nations Conference on Environment and Development (UNCED). The main objective of Agenda 21 is to reduce greenhouse gas emissions, mitigate climate change, and promote sustainable use of natural resources.

Delaney argues that Agenda 21 has been hijacked by powerful interests such as fossil fuel companies, corporations, and governments. He claims that the agreement's focus on economic growth and development at the expense of environmental protection has led to a lack of meaningful action on climate change.

Delaney also points out that many countries have signed up to Agenda 21 without fully understanding its implications or committing to its principles. This is because the agreement was drafted by governments, corporations, and other powerful interests in secret, with little public scrutiny or oversight.

In his view, Agenda 21 has become a tool for maintaining power and control over vulnerable populations, particularly those living in developing countries. Delaney argues that this is evident in the way it has been used to justify policies such as carbon pricing, greenwashing, and environmental degradation.

Delaney's critique of Agenda 21 highlights its limitations and contradictions. He argues that the agreement does not actually address climate change or sustainable development; instead, it provides a framework for governments and corporations to maintain their power and influence over natural resources.

Overall, Delaney's analysis suggests that Agenda 21 is a flawed concept that has been co-opted by powerful interests to justify environmental degradation and economic growth. He advocates for a more radical approach to addressing climate change, one that prioritizes human rights, social justice, and sustainable development over economic growth.

Delaney's work provides an important critique of the limitations of Agenda 21 and highlights the need for a new framework for addressing global environmental issues. His ideas have inspired many activists and scholars to rethink their understanding of sustainability and its relationship with power and politics. [end of text]
