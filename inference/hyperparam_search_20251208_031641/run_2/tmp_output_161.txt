To understand this question, let's first define what Christianity is.

Christianity is a monotheistic religion that originated in the 1st century AD in the Middle East. It is based on the teachings of Jesus Christ, who lived as a human being and was crucified for the sins of humanity. The core beliefs of Christianity include:

* The Trinity: God exists as one being (Father, Son, Holy Spirit) but also as three distinct persons.
* The Incarnation: Jesus Christ is both fully human and fully divine.
* The Resurrection: Jesus' death and resurrection are seen as a triumph over sin and death.
* The Kingdom of God: A future state where all people will live in harmony with God.

Now, let's address the question "Are all Christians Christian?"

The answer depends on how one defines Christianity. Here are some possible perspectives:

1. **Religious identity**: From this perspective, being a Christian means having a specific set of beliefs and practices that define your faith. In this case, not everyone who identifies as Christian is automatically considered a Christian.
2. **Cultural context**: Some people might view Christianity as an exclusive or dominant culture, implying that only those from certain backgrounds are truly Christians. This perspective overlooks the diversity within Christianity and its many denominations.
3. **Personal faith experience**: Many individuals identify themselves as Christians without necessarily adhering to specific theological beliefs or practices. They may have a deep sense of spirituality or connection with God but not necessarily subscribe to traditional Christian doctrine.

However, there are also arguments for why all Christians should be considered:

1. **Shared values and principles**: Christianity shares many commonalities with other faiths, such as the importance of compassion, love, and service.
2. **Common human experiences**: Many people share similar struggles, hopes, and fears that transcend cultural or religious boundaries.
3. **Spiritual significance**: For those who identify as Christians, their faith is a source of guidance, comfort, and inspiration in life.

Ultimately, whether all Christians are considered Christian depends on how one weighs the importance of shared values, common experiences, and spiritual significance. While there may be differences within Christianity, its core principles – such as compassion, love, and redemption – can unite people across cultures and backgrounds.

In conclusion, being a Christian is not just about following specific dogma or practices; it's also about embracing the universal human experience of seeking connection with something greater than oneself.
