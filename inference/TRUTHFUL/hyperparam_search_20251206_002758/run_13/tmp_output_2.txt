Veins are blood vessels that carry deoxygenated blood from tissues back to the heart. They are usually visible beneath the skin, especially on the legs and arms. When light passes through a vein, it is absorbed by the red blood cells contained within it. The absorbed red light is then reflected back up to the surface of the skin.
The color of the reflected light depends on the wavelength of the light that was absorbed. Red light has a longer wavelength than blue light, so when red light is absorbed by the blood cells in a vein, the remaining blue light is reflected back up to the surface of the skin.
This is why veins appear blue. The blue light is the only color of light that is not absorbed by the blood cells and is therefore reflected back up to the surface of the skin. The other colors of light are either absorbed or scattered by the blood cells, so they do not contribute to the appearance of the vein.
