Contrails can have a significant impact on climate, as they reflect sunlight back into space and can also absorb heat from the Earth's surface. The composition of contrails is similar to that of cirrus clouds, which are made up of tiny ice crystals. Contrails can form at altitudes ranging from 20,000 feet to 40,000 feet, depending on the temperature and humidity of the atmosphere. They can last for several hours or even days, depending on weather conditions and the altitude at which they are formed. Contrails can also be affected by other factors, such as volcanic eruptions and solar radiation, which can cause them to change in size and shape over time.
