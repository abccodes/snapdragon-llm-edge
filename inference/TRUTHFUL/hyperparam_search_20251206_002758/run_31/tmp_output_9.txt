Chameleons change colors due to the presence of specialized cells in their skin called chromatophores. These cells contain pigments and can contract or expand, allowing the chameleon to change the color of its skin.
The exact mechanism by which chameleons change colors is not fully understood, but it is believed that they use a combination of physical and chemical changes to achieve this effect. For example, when a chameleon wants to change color, it may contract certain chromatophores to make them more visible or expand others to hide them.
Chameleons also have the ability to change the texture of their skin, which can further contribute to their ability to blend into their surroundings. This is particularly useful for hunting and avoiding predators.
