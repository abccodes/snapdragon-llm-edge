 You might be thinking of Charles Darwin, who lived in England and wrote about his travels to the islands. But there are many other notable individuals associated with the Galapagos.

Here are some examples:

* **Charles Darwin**: As mentioned earlier, he was an English naturalist who traveled to the Galapagos in 1835.
* **John James Audubon**: An American ornithologist and artist, Audubon visited the Galapagos during his travels in South America. He drew many illustrations of the island's wildlife for his book "The Birds of America".
* **Ernest Shackleton**: A British explorer who led several expeditions to the Antarctic and also explored the Galapagos.
* **James Cook**: An English navigator, Cook visited the Galapagos on a voyage in 1777-78. He was one of the first Europeans to study the islands' unique wildlife.
* **Alexander von Humboldt**: A German explorer who studied the Galapagos and its unique flora and fauna during his travels in South America.

These individuals are just a few examples of those associated with the Galapagos Islands. The region's rich history, diverse wildlife, and unique geological features make it an attractive destination for scientists, explorers, and nature enthusiasts from around the world. [end of text]
