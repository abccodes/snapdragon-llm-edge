**The Brain's Allocation**

Research suggests that about 2-3% of the brain's total volume is dedicated to motor control (e.g., movement, coordination). This means that even when we're not actively using our muscles, a significant portion of our brain remains engaged in maintaining motor functions. For example, during sleep, up to 20% of our brain activity is attributed to motor processes.

**The Cerebral Cortex**

The cerebral cortex, the outer layer of the cerebrum responsible for processing sensory information and controlling movement, accounts for approximately 85-90% of the total brain volume (1). This suggests that a significant portion of the brain is dedicated to cognitive functions like perception, attention, memory, language, and emotion regulation.

**The Default Mode Network**

Studies have shown that about 20-30% of brain activity occurs in the default mode network (DMN), which includes regions involved in self-referential thinking, mind-wandering, and introspection. The DMN is thought to be responsible for our sense of self and our ability to reflect on past experiences.

**The Hypothesis**

Considering these findings, a reasonable hypothesis could be that around 20-25% of the brain uses its motor control resources while engaged in everyday activities (e.g., walking, talking). This would leave about 75-80% of the brain free for cognitive processing and other functions like attention, perception, memory, language, emotion regulation, and social cognition.

**Keep in mind**

This is a rough estimate based on various studies. The actual percentage may vary depending on factors such as individual differences, age, and specific tasks or activities.

In conclusion, while we can't pinpoint an exact percentage of brain usage for humans, the available research provides some insight into how our brains allocate their resources.
