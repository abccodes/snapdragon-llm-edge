Here's why:

1. **Mastery of a skill**: Achieving mastery in any domain requires dedication and hard work. When you master a skill or subject, you've demonstrated your ability to apply that knowledge effectively. This process helps build cognitive reserve, which can have numerous benefits for learning and memory.
2. **Neural plasticity**: Research suggests that the brain's neural connections are more malleable during periods of intense focus and practice (Kolb & McEwen, 2011). As you master a skill, your brain undergoes changes in structure and function, which can lead to improved cognitive flexibility and enhanced learning.
3. **Transfer of knowledge**: When you achieve mastery in one area, it's easier to transfer that knowledge to other areas. This is known as the "mastery effect" (Kornell et al., 2004). By applying what you've learned in a different context, you can build cognitive flexibility and improve your ability to learn new information.
4. **Improved working memory**: Mastery of a skill requires sustained attention and focus over time. This helps strengthen working memory, which is essential for learning new material (Baddeley et al., 2000).
5. **Enhanced creativity**: The process of mastering a skill can also foster creativity. By applying what you've learned, you're forced to think outside the box and come up with innovative solutions, which can lead to new ideas and insights.

In conclusion, achieving mastery in sports or any other domain is indeed linked to improved cognitive abilities, including better learning outcomes, enhanced working memory, and increased creative potential.
