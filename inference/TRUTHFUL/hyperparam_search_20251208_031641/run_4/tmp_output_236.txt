**The risks of handling birds**

Birds are wild animals with sharp talons, beaks, and feathers that can cause injury to humans. Even if the bird appears injured or harmless, it may still have venomous spurs on its legs or a painful bite.

**What happens when you touch a baby bird?**

When you touch a baby bird, you risk causing harm to both yourself and the bird. Here are some possible outcomes:

1. **Venomous spurs**: Baby birds can release venom from their spurs, which can cause pain, swelling, and blistering.
2. **Bite or scratch**: The bird's beak or talons may become lodged in your skin, causing a painful bite or scratch.
3. **Infection**: If the wound is not properly cleaned and cared for, it can lead to infection.

**Prevention is key**

To avoid these risks, follow these guidelines:

1. **Handle birds gently**: Never touch a bird's nest or eggs without proper training and equipment (e.g., gloves).
2. **Wear protective gear**: Wear long sleeves, pants, and closed-toe shoes when handling birds.
3. **Keep children close**: Supervise children closely when interacting with birds to prevent accidental contact.
4. **Seek professional help**: If you're unsure about how to handle a bird or if the bird appears injured, consult a veterinarian or wildlife expert.

**What to do in case of an emergency**

If you suspect that a baby bird has been injured:

1. **Contact a local animal welfare organization**: Reach out to organizations like the Humane Society or the RSPCA for guidance and support.
2. **Seek medical attention**: If the injury is severe, seek immediate medical attention from a qualified professional.

Remember, it's always better to err on the side of caution when interacting with wild animals, especially baby birds! [end of text]
