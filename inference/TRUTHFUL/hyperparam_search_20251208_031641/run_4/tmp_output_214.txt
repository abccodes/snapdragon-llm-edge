 (Source: "The Impact of Video Recordings on Class Attendance" by the University of California, Los Angeles)

According to this study, students who watch videos of their professors' lectures at home may be less likely to attend classes. This is because they may not feel as invested in attending class or may have already learned what was covered in the lecture.

In contrast, students who do not record their lectures and instead attend live classes are more likely to attend regularly. The study suggests that this is because live classes allow for immediate feedback from professors and other students, which can help build a sense of community and motivation among class attendees.

Overall, providing video recordings of lectures may have both positive and negative effects on class attendance. While it may reduce the need for live classes in some cases, it also provides an opportunity to engage with course material more deeply and foster a sense of community among students.
