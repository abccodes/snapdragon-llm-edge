1. **Short-term memory**: When we first read something, it enters our short-term memory (also known as working memory) for a few seconds. This is where we process and store the initial information.
2. **Long-term memory**: As we continue reading, our long-term memory takes over to hold the majority of the information. However, research suggests that about 80% of what we read is forgotten within 24 hours after finishing it.
3. **Repetition effect**: The more times you repeat a piece of text or information, the stronger your memory for it becomes. This is known as the repetition effect.
4. **Contextual influence**: Our memories are often influenced by context and other factors, such as emotions, associations, and prior experiences. These contextual influences can significantly impact how we remember what we've read.
5. **Emotional connection**: When we're emotionally invested in a piece of writing or information (e.g., it's a personal experience), our memory for it is stronger. This emotional connection can make us more likely to recall specific details and events.
