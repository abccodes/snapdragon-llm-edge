 This question is a classic example of a philosophical inquiry into the nature of existence, identity, and reality. It raises fundamental questions about human consciousness, selfhood, and the concept of "being." In this essay, we will explore the various perspectives on this question, including Plato's theory of forms, Aristotle's concept of potentiality and actuality, and Jean-Paul Sartre's existentialism.

## Step 1: Understanding the Question
The question "Who was the first man ever?" is a complex inquiry that touches upon various aspects of human existence. It involves considering the nature of identity, consciousness, and reality itself. This type of question has been debated by philosophers for centuries, leading to diverse perspectives on its meaning.

## Step 2: Plato's Theory of Forms
Plato proposed that there existed a realm of abstract forms or ideas that were more fundamental than the physical world. According to this theory, the first man was not just any ordinary human being but rather an embodiment of one of these abstract forms. In other words, he was a perfect representation of his ideal self.

## Step 3: Aristotle's Concept of Potentiality and Actuality
Aristotle believed that potentiality (dunamis) is a state or condition that something can be in at any given time, while actuality (energeia) refers to the realization or manifestation of this potential. In his work "Metaphysics," Aristotle argued that humans are potential beings and that our first existence was as potential human beings.

## Step 4: Jean-Paul Sartre's Existentialism
Sartre posited that human existence is characterized by freedom and responsibility, with no inherent meaning or purpose. According to existentialist philosophy, the concept of "being" becomes a matter of individual choice and creation. In this sense, humans are not predetermined beings but rather free agents who must take responsibility for their choices.

## Step 5: Conclusion
The question "Who was the first man ever?" raises fundamental questions about identity, consciousness, reality, and existence. Each philosophical perspective offers a unique understanding of these concepts, highlighting the complexity and diversity of human experience. By examining various perspectives on this inquiry, we can gain insight into the nature of human existence and our place within the world.

The final answer is: There is no single "right" answer to this question as it depends on one's philosophical perspective. However, by exploring different viewpoints, such as Plato's theory of forms, Aristotelian concept of potentiality, and existentialist ideas from Sartre, we can gain a deeper understanding of the complexities involved in defining human existence.
