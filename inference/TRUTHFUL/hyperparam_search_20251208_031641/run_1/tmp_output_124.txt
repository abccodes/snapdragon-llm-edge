Tap water safety depends on several factors, including:

1. **Source**: Tap water comes from various sources, such as rivers, lakes, reservoirs, or municipal supplies. Each source can have its own set of contaminants.
2. **Treatment process**: The treatment process used to remove impurities and contaminants can vary greatly between cities and countries.
3. **Regulations**: Local regulations regarding tap water quality may differ significantly from one place to another.

**Common contaminants found in tap water:**

1. **Bacteria (e.g., E. coli, Salmonella)**: Can cause food poisoning
2. **Viruses (e.g., Norovirus, Rotavirus)**: Can lead to gastrointestinal illness
3. **Parasites (e.g., Giardia, Cryptosporidium)**: Can cause diarrhea and other health issues
4. **Chemicals (e.g., PFAS, Lead)**: Can be hazardous to human health
5. **Microplastics**: Small plastic particles can enter the water supply through wastewater treatment plants

**What's being done to improve tap water safety?**

1. **Regulatory updates**: Governments are updating regulations to address emerging contaminants and prioritize public health.
2. **Water quality monitoring**: Regular testing of tap water is conducted to detect potential issues before they become major problems.
3. **Treatment technologies**: New technologies, such as advanced filtration systems and membrane bioreactors, are being developed to improve treatment efficiency and effectiveness.

**What can you do?**

1. **Check your local water utility's website**: Find out what contaminants have been detected in your tap water and how they're being treated.
2. **Use a filter at home**: Consider installing a water filter that meets international standards (e.g., NSF International or WHO recommendations).
3. **Stay informed**: Stay up-to-date with the latest news on tap water safety, as regulations can change over time.

In conclusion, while tap water may not be entirely safe to drink in all cases, it's essential to understand the complexities of its composition and treatment processes.
