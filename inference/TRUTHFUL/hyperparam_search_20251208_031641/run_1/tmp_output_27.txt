The answer is simple: "That's one small step for man, one giant leap for mankind."

However, it turns out that there was another famous phrase attributed to Neil Armstrong, which might seem similar but has a different meaning.

In his book "First Man," published in 2005, astronaut Buzz Aldrin wrote about the day he landed on the Moon. He described saying: "That's one small step... for a man, on the moon."

Aldrin was referring to the fact that he had taken off from the lunar module Eagle and descended back to Earth as part of the Apollo 11 mission. As he entered into lunar orbit, his spacecraft lifted off again, carrying him back towards Earth.

So, while Neil Armstrong's famous phrase "That's one small step" is indeed a reference to taking a giant leap for mankind, it was actually an expression of excitement and accomplishment at having successfully landed on the Moon.

In other words, Buzz Aldrin wasn't saying that he took off from the Moon; he was simply describing his experience as part of a larger mission. The phrase "one small step" is more about the achievement itself than its origin or context.

So, to summarize: Neil Armstrong said "That's one small step," and Buzz Aldrin said "That's one small step." Both phrases are correct, but they have different meanings! [end of text]
