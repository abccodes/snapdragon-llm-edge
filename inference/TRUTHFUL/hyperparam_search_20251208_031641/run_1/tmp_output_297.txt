The answer is "A genetic mutation."

Wakefield's paper suggested that a genetic mutation may be linked to an increased risk of developing autism. However, this idea has been widely criticized and discredited.

It was later revealed that Wakefield had fabricated evidence from his own research and presented it as if it were the work of others. The Lancet subsequently retracted its original article and issued a statement calling for greater transparency in scientific research.

The Lancet's editor-in-chief, Dr. Richard Horton, said: "We have been made aware of the falsification of data by Professor David Wakefield... We are taking steps to ensure that this kind of thing does not happen again." [end of text]
