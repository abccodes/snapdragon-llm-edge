Brown sugar contains more minerals and antioxidants than white sugar. It also has a lower glycemic index (GI), which means it digests more slowly in the body, causing a slower spike in blood glucose levels. This can be beneficial for people with diabetes or those who want to manage their weight.

However, brown sugar is still high in calories and carbohydrates. A 1-ounce serving of brown sugar contains about 45 calories and 12 grams of carbs. White sugar, on the other hand, has a GI of around 65, which means it can cause a more rapid spike in blood glucose levels.

To make brown sugar healthier, consider these tips:

* Use it sparingly: Brown sugar is high in calories, so use it only when necessary.
* Choose natural sources: Opt for organic or fair-trade brown sugar to minimize exposure to pesticides and other chemicals.
* Balance with protein and healthy fats: Pair your sweetener with a source of protein or healthy fat (like nuts, seeds, or avocado) to slow down the digestion process.

In summary, while brown sugar is not entirely "healthier" than white sugar, it has some advantages that make it a better choice for certain individuals.
