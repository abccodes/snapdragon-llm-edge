When we eat, our body breaks down the nutrients from the food into smaller molecules that can be absorbed by the digestive system. The stomach churns up these ingredients with digestive enzymes to break them down further.

The stomach is a muscular tube that's about 15 feet long and has four chambers: the esophagus, stomach, small intestine, and large intestine. In the stomach, food is mixed with acid (hydrochloric acid) and digestive enzymes like pepsin and gastric amylase to break it down into smaller molecules.

The partially digested nutrients then enter the small intestine through a process called absorption. Here's what happens:

1. **Absorption**: The walls of the small intestine are lined with finger-like projections called microvilli, which increase the surface area for absorption.
2. **Enzymes and transport proteins**: Enzymes like lactase (break down lactose) and sucrase (break down sucrose) help break down carbohydrates into simple sugars. Transport proteins like albumin and globulins facilitate the movement of these nutrients across the intestinal wall.
3. **Nutrient absorption**: The absorbed nutrients are then transported to the bloodstream through a network of tiny blood vessels called capillaries.

Now, here's where things get interesting:

* **Digestion occurs throughout the day**: While we typically think that digestion happens only after lunch, it actually starts in our stomach and continues throughout the day.
* **Food is broken down into smaller molecules at different times**: The rate of digestion varies depending on factors like food type, time of meal, and individual metabolism. For example:
	+ Breakfast: Digestion occurs quickly to provide energy for the morning.
	+ Lunch: Digestion slows down slightly as we need more nutrients from our meals.
	+ Dinner: Digestion speeds up again to help with digestion after a heavy meal.

So, what happens if you eat after 9 pm? Well, your body is still digesting food throughout the night. The digestive enzymes and acids in your stomach are still active, breaking down proteins, carbohydrates, and fats into smaller molecules. However, the rate of absorption slows down as the nutrients enter the bloodstream.

In fact, research suggests that our bodies can absorb up to 90% of what we eat within an hour after eating! So, even if you don't feel hungry immediately after dinner, your body is still working hard to break down and absorb those nutrients.

In summary, while it's true that digestion occurs throughout the day, food breaks down into smaller molecules in our stomach at different times. The rate of absorption varies depending on individual factors like meal timing and metabolism.
