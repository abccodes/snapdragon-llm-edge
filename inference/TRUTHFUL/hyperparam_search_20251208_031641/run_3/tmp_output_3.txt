 You might be surprised to find that it's not actually the hottest, but rather the one with the most seeds.

Chili peppers are typically divided into three main categories: sweet, hot, and spicy. The 'spicy' category refers to peppers that have more than 100,000 Scoville heat units (SHU), which is a measure of how spicy they are. However, not all chili peppers fall under this category.

The hottest part of the pepper is actually the placenta, which contains most of the capsaicinoids responsible for the spiciness. Capsaicinoids are chemical compounds that bind to pain receptors in the mouth and throat, causing a burning sensation when ingested.

On the other hand, peppers with fewer seeds tend to be milder. This is because more seeds contain less capsaicinoid, which reduces their overall heat level. For example, bell peppers have very few seeds (about 0-1) while jalapenos have around 200 SHU per seed!

So there you have it! The spiciest part of the chili pepper isn't actually the hottest - but rather the one with more seeds!" [end of text]
