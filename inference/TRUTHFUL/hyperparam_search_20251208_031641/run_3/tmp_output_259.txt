## Step 1: Understanding the Basics of Cat Physiology
Cats are known for their remarkable adaptability and resilience. They can live in a wide range of temperatures, from -40°C to 38°C (-40°F to 100°F), which is quite impressive considering their small size. However, this adaptability comes with some limitations.

## Step 2: Identifying the Limitations of Cats
One major limitation of cats is their sensitivity to cold and heat. They are not as tolerant of extreme temperatures as other animals. For example, a cat that spends its entire life in a warm environment will quickly succumb to hypothermia if exposed to cold temperatures.

## Step 3: Considering the Impact of Environmental Factors
Cats also have limited ability to adapt to certain environmental factors such as humidity and altitude. They can tolerate low humidity but are sensitive to high altitudes, which can lead to respiratory problems.

## Step 4: Examining the Role of Genetics in Cat Survival
Genetics play a significant role in determining how well cats can survive in various environments. Some breeds are more resilient than others due to their genetic makeup. For example, some breeds have been bred for specific climates and can thrive in those conditions.

## Step 5: Understanding the Concept of "Survival" vs. "Adaptation"
While cats can adapt to certain environments, they may not always survive or thrive in them. Survival refers to the ability to live through a particular period without immediate harm, whereas adaptation refers to the process by which an organism becomes better suited to its environment.

## Step 6: Recognizing the Importance of Human Intervention
In many cases, cats are rescued from environments that would be hostile to their survival or well-being. This highlights the importance of human intervention in ensuring the safety and health of domesticated animals like cats.
