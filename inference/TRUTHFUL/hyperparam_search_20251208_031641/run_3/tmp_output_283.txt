The American College of Obstetricians and Gynecologists (ACOG) states that "fetal heart rates should be monitored regularly to assess fetal well-being." However, a normal range for a fetus's heart rate can vary from 110-160 beats per minute. A rapid or irregular heartbeat may indicate potential issues with the baby.

The American Academy of Pediatrics recommends monitoring fetal heart rates as follows:

* Normal: 100-140 beats per minute
* Abnormal (low): less than 90 beats per minute
* Abnormal (high): more than 160 beats per minute

If a healthcare provider suspects that a fetus may be in distress, they will typically perform an ultrasound to assess the baby's well-being. This can provide valuable information about potential issues and guide treatment decisions.

In some cases, additional tests or procedures may be necessary to confirm fetal viability or detect any underlying conditions. These may include:

* Non-stress test (NST): measures the baby's heart rate in response to movement
* Biophysical profile (BPP): evaluates the baby's movements, tone, and breathing patterns
* Amniocentesis: analyzes the amniotic fluid for genetic material

Early detection of fetal distress is crucial to ensure the best possible outcome for both the mother and the baby. If you have concerns about your pregnancy or suspect that a fetus may be at risk, consult with your healthcare provider as soon as possible.
