 According to the American Cancer Society, breast cancer is the leading cause of death among women aged 35-44 and a leading cause of deaths among all women.
The top five causes of death for women in this age group are:
1. Heart disease
2. Lung cancer
3. Breast cancer
4. Chronic lower respiratory diseases (including chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, or COPD)
5. Accidents (unintentional injuries)

Breast cancer is the most common type of female cancer and accounts for about 11% of all new cases in the United States each year.

The American Cancer Society recommends that women start getting regular mammograms at age 40 to reduce their risk of developing breast cancer. The organization also suggests that women with a family history of breast cancer or those who have had previous radiation exposure should undergo more frequent screening, such as every three years starting at age 45 and then annually.

In addition to these recommendations, the American Cancer Society emphasizes the importance of maintaining a healthy lifestyle, including:

* Eating a balanced diet rich in fruits, vegetables, whole grains, and lean protein sources
* Staying physically active through regular exercise or other forms of physical activity
* Not smoking or using tobacco products
* Limiting alcohol consumption to moderate levels (up to one drink per day for women)
* Getting enough sleep each night

By following these recommendations and staying informed about breast cancer risks, women can reduce their chances of developing this disease. [end of text]
