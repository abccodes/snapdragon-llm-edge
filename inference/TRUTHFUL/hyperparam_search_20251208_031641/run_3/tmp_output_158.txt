## Step 1: Understanding the Question
The question "Who was the first man ever?" implies that we are looking for an individual who existed before any other humans. However, this raises questions about what constitutes "first" in terms of human existence or development.

## Step 2: Considering Historical Perspectives
Historically, many cultures and civilizations have been considered as the first humans due to their supposed origins from a mythical figure or being thought to be the originator of humanity. Examples include Adam and Eve in Christianity, Cain and Abel in biblical mythology, and Prometheus in Greek mythology.

## Step 3: Examining Scientific Perspectives
From a scientific standpoint, the concept of "first" is often tied to evolutionary biology. The first humans are believed to have evolved from apes around 6-8 million years ago during the Pleistocene epoch. This implies that there was no single individual who existed as humanity's first ancestor.

## Step 4: Considering Philosophical Perspectives
Philosophers have debated the concept of "first" in various contexts, including the origin of time itself and the nature of existence. Some argue that the concept of a singular "first" is problematic because it implies a linear progression from nothing to something, which may not be supported by our current understanding of cosmology.

## Step 5: Synthesizing Information
Considering all these perspectives, it's clear that defining the first human as an individual with no prior existence or influence on subsequent humans is challenging. The concept of "first" in this context seems more like a matter of historical and cultural significance rather than a scientifically defined term.

The final answer is: $\boxed{There is no definitive answer to who was the first man ever, as it depends on how one defines "first."}$ [end of text]
