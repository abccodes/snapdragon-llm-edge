 No, it is not. A heart attack and a cardiac arrest are two different conditions.

A heart attack occurs when the coronary arteries (the blood vessels that supply oxygen to your heart muscle) become blocked or narrowed due to plaque buildup, which can be caused by high cholesterol, smoking, diabetes, and other risk factors. This blockage causes damage to the heart tissue, leading to a range of symptoms such as chest pain, shortness of breath, fatigue, and nausea.

A cardiac arrest occurs when the heart suddenly stops beating or beats too slowly for an extended period (usually more than 4 minutes). During a cardiac arrest, the body's vital functions are severely impaired, including breathing, circulation, and blood pressure. The cause of a cardiac arrest can be related to various factors such as high blood pressure, heart disease, stroke, or other medical conditions.

In summary, while both heart attacks and cardiac arrests involve damage to the heart muscle, they have distinct causes and symptoms. A heart attack is typically caused by a blockage in one of the coronary arteries, whereas a cardiac arrest occurs when there's an interruption in the normal functioning of the heart."
