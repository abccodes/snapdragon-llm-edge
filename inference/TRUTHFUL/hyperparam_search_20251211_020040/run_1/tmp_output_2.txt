## Step 1: Understand how light interacts with objects
When light passes through an object, it can be absorbed, reflected, or transmitted depending on its properties such as color and wavelength.

## Step 2: Consider the role of blood in the body's circulatory system
Blood is a clear liquid that transports oxygen and nutrients to various parts of the body while removing waste products. It also carries red blood cells which contain hemoglobin, a protein that binds with oxygen.

## Step 3: Analyze how light interacts with blood vessels
When light enters the body through the skin or eyes, it encounters blood vessels in the form of capillaries (small blood vessels). The blue color we see is due to the scattering of light by the hemoglobin in red blood cells.

## Step 4: Explain why veins appear blue under normal conditions
Veins are essentially two-way valves that allow blood to flow upwards towards the heart. Under normal conditions, when light enters a vein and encounters the red blood cells with their bound hemoglobin, it is scattered back into the surrounding air or other mediums, giving it its characteristic blue color.

## Step 5: Consider any exceptions
There are exceptions where veins can appear more greenish due to certain medical conditions such as jaundice (yellowing of the skin and eyes) caused by elevated levels of bilirubin in the blood. However, this is not a common occurrence.
