In 1941, Japanese immigrants brought their custom to America and introduced them to the American people. They were initially used in Chinese restaurants but eventually became popular among Americans. These fortunes were often printed on small paper slips with messages that could be slipped into a cookie as an afterthought. The name "fortune cookies" was coined by a food manufacturer, Fong's Bakery, which began using the term to market their product.
