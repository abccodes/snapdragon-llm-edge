After learning that the sun is a massive ball of hot, glowing gas, I realized that its surface temperature is about 5,500 degrees Celsius (10,000 degrees Fahrenheit). This heat energy is what gives the sun its characteristic yellowish color.

But why does it appear white when viewed from Earth? The answer lies in the way light interacts with our atmosphere. When sunlight enters Earth's atmosphere, it encounters tiny molecules of gases like nitrogen and oxygen. These molecules scatter the shorter wavelengths of light, such as blue and violet, more than the longer wavelengths, like red and yellow.

This phenomenon is known as Rayleigh scattering, named after the British physicist Lord Rayleigh who first described it in the late 19th century. As a result, our atmosphere scatters sunlight in a way that makes it appear white to us on Earth.

In space, however, where there are no air molecules to scatter light, the sun appears more like a bright yellowish disk. This is because the surface temperature of the sun is so high that it emits light across a wide range of wavelengths, including red and orange.

So, my friend and I were correct in our initial question: "What color is the sun when viewed from Earth?" The answer is white, but its appearance changes depending on the angle of view and the amount of atmospheric scattering present. In space, it appears yellowish due to Rayleigh scattering.
