**Brain Structure and Function**

The human brain is divided into several distinct regions, each with unique roles:

1. **Cerebrum**: The largest part of the brain, responsible for processing sensory information, controlling movement, managing higher-level cognitive processes (e.g., thought, emotion).
2. **Cerebellum**: Located at the base of the brain, it coordinates muscle movements and maintains balance.
3. **Brainstem**: Connecting the cerebrum to the spinal cord, this region regulates basic functions like breathing, heart rate, and blood pressure.

**Which Areas Use Most Brain Power?**

Research suggests that different areas use varying percentages of the brain's overall capacity:

* **Cerebral cortex**: The outer layer of the brain responsible for processing sensory information (around 85-90% of total brain power). This area is divided into four main regions: frontal, parietal, temporal, and occipital.
* **Basal ganglia**: A group of structures involved in movement control, habit formation, and reward-based learning. They use around 5-10% of the brain's capacity.

**Other Key Regions**

Some other notable areas that contribute to various cognitive functions:

* **Hippocampus**: Essential for forming and consolidating memories (around 2-3% of total brain power).
* **Amygdala**: Involved in emotional processing, fear response, and memory consolidation (around 1-2% of total brain capacity).

**Keep in Mind:**

The percentages mentioned above are approximate and based on various studies. Brain function is highly complex, and the actual usage can vary depending on factors like age, sex, and specific cognitive tasks.

In conclusion, while we can't pinpoint an exact percentage for which part of the brain "uses" 100% capacity, it's clear that different regions contribute to distinct functions.
