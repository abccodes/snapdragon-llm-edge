Veins are blood vessels that carry deoxygenated blood from our tissues back to the heart. They have thinner walls than arteries, which allows them to expand when they fill with blood. This expansion causes the blood inside the vein to be pushed against the inner lining of the vessel, creating a pulsating effect as it flows.
When light enters our eyes, it is absorbed by various pigments in our retina and converted into electrical signals that are sent to our brain for processing. The color we perceive depends on how much light is reflected back from an object or surface.
Veins appear blue because the blood inside them contains a pigment called hemoglobin, which absorbs red light but reflects blue light. When this blue light passes through the skin and tissues surrounding the vein, it reaches our eyes where we see it as blue.
