The reason for this is that these parts contain capsaicin, which is responsible for the spicy taste of chili peppers. Capsaicin is a chemical compound found naturally in chili peppers and other plants such as cayenne pepper, paprika, and hot sauce.
Capsaicin works by stimulating nerve endings in the mouth and throat, causing a burning sensation that we perceive as heat or spiciness. The intensity of this effect varies depending on the concentration of capsaicin in the chili pepper.
The seeds and membranes contain more capsaicin than other parts of the chili pepper, which is why they are often considered to be the hottest part of the plant. However, it's worth noting that even the outer skin of a chili pepper can contain some capsaicin and may cause a burning sensation when touched.
