The reason for this is that these parts contain capsaicin, which is responsible for the spicy taste of chili peppers. Capsaicin is a chemical compound found naturally in chili peppers and other plants in the nightshade family.
Capsaicin works by stimulating nerve endings in the mouth and throat, causing them to send signals to the brain that trigger a sensation of heat or pain. This is why eating hot chili peppers can make your tongue feel like it's on fire!
The spiciest part of the chili pepper varies depending on the variety and breed of chili pepper. Some varieties have more capsaicin than others, making them hotter than others.
For example, habanero peppers are considered some of the hottest chili peppers in the world, with a heat level that can range from 100,000 to over one million Scoville Heat Units (SHU). On the other hand, bell peppers have virtually no capsaicin and are not spicy at all.
