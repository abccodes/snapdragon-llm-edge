Veins are blood vessels that carry deoxygenated blood from different parts of the body back to the heart. They have thinner walls compared to arteries, which means they can expand more easily when filled with blood. As a result, veins appear blue because they absorb red wavelengths of light and reflect blue wavelengths back to our eyes.
When we look at an object, our brain interprets the colors it sees based on how much energy is reflected from that object. For example, objects that are red appear bright and vibrant because they reflect more red wavelengths than other colors. In contrast, objects that absorb red wavelengths appear dark or dull because they do not reflect as many of those wavelengths back to our eyes.
