The professor's reasoning: If the penny falls straight down and hits your head at 108 miles per hour (mph), which is its terminal velocity, it will exert a force of about 64 pounds. That may sound like a lot, but compared to other forces you encounter daily, it’s not too bad.

For example, when you jump from a building or fall off your bike, the impact can be much more severe because your body has less time to absorb the shock. And even if you were hit by a falling penny at 10 mph (mph), which would happen if it fell straight down from about 3 feet high, that force is still only about one-third of what you experience when you fall off a bike.
