Veins are blood vessels that carry deoxygenated blood from tissues back to the heart. They are usually visible beneath the skin, especially on the legs and arms. When light passes through a vein, some of it is absorbed by the blood in the vessel, while the rest is reflected back to our eyes. The color we perceive depends on the wavelengths of light that are reflected.
Blue light has shorter wavelengths than other colors of light, so it is more easily absorbed by the blood in a vein. As a result, less blue light is reflected back to our eyes, and we perceive the vein as blue.
