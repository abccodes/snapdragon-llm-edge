Veins are blood vessels that carry deoxygenated blood from tissues back to the heart. They are usually visible beneath the skin, especially on the legs and arms. When light passes through a vein, some of it is absorbed by the blood in the vessel, while the rest is reflected back to our eyes. The color we perceive depends on the wavelengths of light that are reflected.
Red light has shorter wavelengths than blue light, so when red light passes through a vein, most of it is absorbed by the blood and only a small amount is reflected back to our eyes. Blue light, on the other hand, has longer wavelengths than red light, so when it passes through a vein, more of it is reflected back to our eyes.
This is why veins appear blue. The blue color we see is due to the reflection of blue light from the blood in the vein. The blue light is scattered by the surrounding tissue and reflects back to our eyes, giving the appearance of a blue vein.
