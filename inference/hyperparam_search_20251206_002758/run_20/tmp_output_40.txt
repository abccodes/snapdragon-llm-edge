Aircraft leave behind trails in the sky that are composed of various substances, including water vapor, carbon dioxide, and nitrogen oxides. These substances are released into the atmosphere when the engines of the aircraft burn fuel.
Water vapor is the most common substance found in these trails, and it is produced by the combustion of fuel in the engine. As the water vapor cools and condenses, it forms clouds that can be seen from the ground.
Carbon dioxide and nitrogen oxides are also released into the atmosphere when the engines of the aircraft burn fuel. These substances contribute to the greenhouse effect and can have an impact on climate change.
In addition to these substances, some aircraft also release other materials into the atmosphere, such as soot and ash. Soot is produced by incomplete combustion of fuel, while ash is produced by volcanic eruptions or other sources.
