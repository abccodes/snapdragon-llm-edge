The spiciness of chili peppers comes from a chemical compound called capsaicin, which is found in the flesh and seeds of the fruit. Capsaicin is what gives chili peppers their heat and makes them so spicy.
When you eat a chili pepper, the capsaicin binds to pain receptors in your mouth and nose, causing a burning sensation. This sensation is often described as "hot" or "spicy."
The seeds of chili peppers contain more capsaicin than the flesh, which is why they are generally considered to be the spiciest part of the fruit. However, the heat level of a chili pepper can vary depending on the variety and how it is grown.
In general, the hotter a chili pepper is, the more capsaicin it contains. Some varieties of chili peppers, such as habanero and ghost peppers, are known for their intense heat and high levels of capsaicin.
