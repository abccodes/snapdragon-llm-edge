An earthworm is a segmented worm that lives in soil and helps to aerate the soil and break down organic matter. Earthworms are important for maintaining healthy soil and supporting plant growth.
If an earthworm is cut in half, it will not survive. The head and tail of the earthworm contain vital organs and tissues that allow it to function. When an earthworm is cut in half, the head and tail will no longer be able to communicate with each other, and the body will begin to deteriorate.
The cut ends of the earthworm will also begin to rot and decay. This is because the cells in the cut ends are no longer receiving nutrients and oxygen from the rest of the body. The cut ends may also attract bacteria and other pathogens that can cause further damage to the earthworm.
