The quote "Only the dead have seen the end" is often attributed to Winston Churchill. However, it has been suggested that this may not be accurate and there are other sources who attribute it to different individuals.

One possible source for the quote is a speech given by General Douglas MacArthur in 1950 during the Korean War. In his speech, he said: "The only thing we have to fear is fear itself." This has been interpreted as a reference to Churchill's famous line about war and death.

Another possibility is that the quote was first used by British author H.G. Wells in his science fiction novel "War of the Worlds" published in 1897. In this book, he wrote: "Only those who have seen death can understand life." This has been interpreted as a metaphor for war and its toll on human lives.

Regardless of its origin, the quote remains an enduring reminder of the devastating impact of war and the importance of remembering those who have lost their lives in conflict. [end of text]
