The trails left in the atmosphere by airplanes are primarily composed of water vapor, along with small amounts of other substances like nitrogen oxides. These trails form as a result of the combustion process that powers the engines of commercial jets. As fuel burns inside the engine, it produces hot gases that expand rapidly and cool down upon contact with the surrounding air, forming clouds or contrails. The composition and appearance of these trails can vary depending on factors such as altitude, temperature, and humidity.
In summary, aircraft trails are primarily composed of water vapor and nitrogen oxides, formed by the combustion process in the engine. [end of text]
