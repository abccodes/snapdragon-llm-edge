What Happens If You Swallow Gum?
When you swallow gum, it usually passes through your digestive system without causing any harm. However, in some cases, swallowing gum can lead to serious problems.
One of the most common issues associated with swallowing gum is blockages. As gum travels down your esophagus and into your stomach, it may become lodged in a narrow part of your digestive tract. This can cause severe pain and discomfort, as well as difficulty breathing and vomiting.
In rare cases, swallowing large amounts of gum can lead to perforations or tears in the lining of your digestive tract. These injuries can be life-threatening and require immediate medical attention.
Other potential consequences of swallowing gum include:
- Digestive issues: Swallowing gum can cause indigestion, bloating, and diarrhea.
- Gum disease: If you swallow large amounts of gum, it can accumulate on your teeth and contribute to gum disease.
- Allergic reactions: Some people may be allergic to the ingredients in gum, which can cause an allergic reaction if swallowed.
How to Deal with Swallowed Gum
If you swallow a small piece of gum, there is usually no need for concern. The gum will likely pass through your digestive system within a few hours without causing any problems.
However, if you experience severe symptoms such as pain, difficulty breathing, or vomiting after swallowing gum, it's important to seek medical attention immediately.
In some cases, doctors may recommend that you undergo an endoscopy to remove the gum from your digestive tract. During this procedure, a small camera is inserted into your esophagus through your mouth and throat. The doctor can then use specialized tools to retrieve the gum and prevent any further complications.
If you swallow large amounts of gum or experience severe symptoms after swallowing it, it's important to contact emergency services immediately. In some cases, surgery may be necessary to remove the gum from your body.
Preventing Swallowed Gum
While it's impossible to completely prevent swallowing gum, there are several steps you can take to reduce your risk:
- Keep gum out of reach of children and pets: Small pieces of gum can be dangerous for young children and pets, so keep them away from these individuals.
- Avoid eating gum in public: Eating gum in public can lead to accidental ingestion, especially if you're not paying close attention to what you're eating.
- Dispose of gum properly: Make sure to dispose of used gum properly, either by putting it in a trash can or recycling bin.
