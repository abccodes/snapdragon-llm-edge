Veins are blood vessels that carry deoxygenated blood back to the heart from the rest of the body. They are usually thinner than arteries, which carry oxygen-rich blood away from the heart to the rest of the body.
When light enters a vein, some of it is absorbed by the tissue surrounding the vessel and some of it reflects off the walls of the vessel. The color that we see when looking at a vein depends on how much light is reflected back to our eyes.
Veins appear blue because they reflect more red light than other colors.
