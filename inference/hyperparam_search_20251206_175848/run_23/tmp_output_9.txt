Chameleons are reptiles found primarily in Africa, Madagascar, and parts of Asia. They have an incredible ability to change their coloration to match their surroundings. This adaptation helps them blend into their environment and avoid predators.
The reason why chameleons can change colors is due to a unique structure called chromatophores. Chromatophores are specialized cells that contain pigments, such as melanin, carotenoids, and phycobilins. These pigments give the chameleon its coloration.
Chromatophores work in conjunction with muscles that control their size and shape. When the muscles contract, they push the pigment deeper into the skin, making the chameleon appear darker. Conversely, when the muscles relax, the pigment is pushed closer to the surface of the skin, causing the chameleon to appear lighter.
Chameleons also have a special layer called the iridium layer that reflects light and gives them their distinctive coloration. This layer helps them absorb sunlight for warmth and reflect it away from predators.
