Veins are blood vessels that carry deoxygenated blood back to the heart from all over the body. They are usually thinner than arteries, which carry oxygen-rich blood away from the heart to the rest of the body.
The color of veins is determined by the way light travels through them and how it interacts with the surrounding tissues. When light enters a vein, it passes through the walls of the vessel and reflects off the red blood cells inside. The blue color we see in veins is actually caused by the reflection of this light from the red blood cells.
The reason why veins appear blue rather than red or green is because they are usually located closer to the surface of the skin than arteries, which means that more light can pass through them and be reflected back towards our eyes. This makes it easier for us to see the blue color of the veins.
