Veins are blood vessels that carry deoxygenated blood from tissues back to the heart. They are usually smaller than arteries, which carry oxygen-rich blood away from the heart to tissues throughout the body. Veins have thinner walls than arteries and contain valves that help prevent blood from flowing backward into them.
When light enters our bodies, it travels through different parts of our skin and tissue layers until it reaches the veins. The color we see depends on how much light is absorbed or reflected by these layers. In general, blue light has a shorter wavelength and higher energy than red light, so it tends to be more easily absorbed by tissues.
When light enters a vein, some of it will be absorbed by the tissue surrounding the vessel, while other wavelengths may pass through and reflect off the walls of the vein. The reflected light is what we see as blue because our eyes are most sensitive to this wavelength of light.
In addition to their color, veins also have a different texture than arteries. They tend to appear more visible under the skin due to their thinner walls and larger diameter.
